## 4. Unresolved issues, evidence, and recommended design

### P0. Fractional hours are wrongly coupled to one private Job

`LaborMarket.jobs` stores `person_id -> Job`. The hiring pool excludes anyone with an
active private job. A worker assigned a small fraction at one firm cannot match the
remaining hours to another firm; the job ladder transfers the entire relationship
rather than combining contracts. Residual labor can therefore enter JG while private
firms report vacancies.

The final five baselines report:

| Seed | Labor supply | Open vacancies | Underemployed heads | Residual hours |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 293.9 | 182.7 | 42.8 | 24.1 |
| 1 | 298.5 | 207.4 | 24.7 | 13.3 |
| 2 | 310.9 | 221.6 | 39.0 | 22.2 |
| 3 | 296.6 | 235.5 | 38.9 | 22.9 |
| 4 | 301.5 | 212.1 | 27.5 | 14.3 |

A separate same-seed 2191-tick structural probe found:

| Variant | Tail labor fill | Tail JG share | Tail vacancies |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fractional reference | 57.9% | 12.4% | 189.0 |
| No matching friction | 51.2% | 6.65% | 276.4 |
| No job ladder | 40.0% | 12.3% | 390.0 |
| Whole-person jobs | 86.0% | 2.24% | 115.9 |

Disabling matching friction is not sufficient. Switching the frontier directly to
whole-person jobs is also unsafe: the tail consumption-firm count fell from about 149
to 52 and the inflation path changed materially.

**Recommended fix:** represent multiple concurrent employer-hour contracts, or add a
conserving residual-hours matching pass. Reconcile wages, taxes, efficiency units,
tenure, suspension, churn, layoffs, firm bankruptcy, and labor-flow identities per
contract. Add a same-seed multi-job twin before changing the frontier default.

### P0. Zero-bank states breach the deprivation validity boundary

Under the new public-capital anchors, the legacy v13 ten-year baseline reaches a
14.4% destitution peak. At that peak all commercial banks are gone and JG uses about
19.1% of labor supply. Removing public-capital productivity lowers the peak to 5.6%;
lowering JG capital productivity lowers it to 5.3%; both still coincide with banking
failure. The old `0.10/0.30` public-capital parameters suppress the early peak, but
also drive per-capita output to about 2.14 and the price index to about 0.235. The old
calibration was masking financial fragility with an excessive productivity subsidy.

The deprivation gauge is not a mortality mechanism. Once the sticky boundary fires,
later demographic and long-run conclusions are out of domain even though
distributional observations remain available.

**Recommended next probe:** trace payment continuity, deposits, credit supply, bank
entry, safety-net cash, and household basket coverage when `banks_alive` reaches zero.
Define whether a zero-bank economy is forbidden, resolved by bridge-bank entry, or an
explicit terminal failure. Do not restore a calendar-based assertion such as "the
first three active years must be quiet."

### P0. Private capital uses an inconsistent daily clock

The long-run private-capital probe reports private capital / annual real GDP near
`0.004`, despite a configured `v=2.5`. Multiplying only `v` by 365 produces roughly
51% investment/GDP and about -31% growth. Scaling initial capital and production
parameters together can raise the capital/output ratio to about 3.9, but produced
roughly 542% CPI YoY in the probe.

**Recommended fix:** treat this as a joint time-unit migration. Re-derive initial
capital, production-function scale, desired-capital rules, capital-goods sector
capacity, finance, depreciation, and any stock/annual-flow comparisons together.
Do not patch `v` locally.

### P1. Energy rationing is not yet cleanly attributed

The final five baselines all report persistent rationing. Tail total orders are about
47.6--60.2 units, capacity is about 53.7--66.5, measured capacity utilization is only
65%--79%, realized investment equals the small target (about 0.02--0.04 units), and
the reported unfilled share is 11%--31%.

These final figures are important: they do **not** support a simple "capacity is at
99%, increase capacity" conclusion. Unfilled orders coexist with apparent headroom.
The remaining attribution must separate budget-feasible physical demand, buyer cash,
matching/allocation, capacity definition, input restocking, and investment response.

**Recommended next probe:** add budget-feasible orders and an exact order identity:
submitted quantity = cash-rejected + matching-rejected + capacity-rejected + filled.
Only then revise the energy investment reaction or construction lag.

### P1. Production plans are systematically unrealized

Tail production realization across the final baselines is 60.7%, 61.8%, 58.5%,
55.7%, and 52.4%. Earlier decomposition showed labor fill moving closely with
production realization, cash-constrained firm shares around 8%--19%, and an
additional energy contribution in some seeds.

**Recommended order:** fix or experimentally isolate the single-Job hours boundary,
then rerun the labor/cash/energy attribution. Do not tune production targets merely to
make the realization ratio pass.

### P1. Bank P&L still lacks contractual funding cost

Realized bank P&L now recognizes loan interest, wholesale legs, and credit losses, but
`interest_by_deposits` remains a distribution rule rather than contractual
account-level deposit interest. Operating cost is also incomplete. Net interest
margin and bank-entry profitability are therefore not realistic.

**Recommended fix:** post deposit interest by account and bank, close loan income,
deposit and wholesale funding cost, provisions/losses, operating cost, taxes, and
dividends through one bank capital bridge.

### P1. Loan contracts and household debt are untyped

Each borrower still has one scalar principal that reprices from the live rate.
Contracts lack origination vintage, fixed/floating type, reset date, maturity, and
contract-level arrears. Mortgage, unsecured consumer, and margin principal share the
same borrower debt rail, while the mortgage book is a collateral shadow.

**Recommended fix:** introduce typed loan contracts first, then aggregate them for
cash, regulatory capital, and headline reporting. Preserve lender ownership and
purpose during inheritance, household restructuring, refinancing, repayment, and
write-off.

### P1. Collateral has no recovery or legal priority

Priced collateral now constrains lending and solvency, but default has no lien
priority, seizure, sale proceeds, bank recovery, or residual write-off allocation.
The current mechanism is a borrowing-base proxy, not complete secured lending.

**Recommended fix:** implement a conserving liquidation journal separating asset
disposal, senior recovery, unsecured recovery, owner residual, and final bank loss.

### P2. Inventory cost is not matched to sales

The balance sheet prices inventory, but the income statement expenses current
production cash cost. It has no lot or weighted-average cost roll-forward and no COGS
matching to current sales. Period profit should not be calibrated for tax, exit, or
valuation until this is fixed.

## 6. Causal experiment interpretation

The final ten-job matrix has one arm per intervention, all against seed 0. Same-seed
pre-period identity checks passed. Because each intervention has only one valid pair,
effects remain `exploratory_single_seed`.

- The rate-cut first stage failed because the baseline policy rate was already at the
  ZLB. The easing IRF is unidentified, not evidence of no monetary channel.
- Credit tightening moved `credit_kappa`, but the reported macro and loan responses
  were effectively zero in that window; the borrower constraint was locally
  nonbinding.
- Earlier three-replicate rate-hike runs passed the first stage and lowered investment
  targets, but GDP and consumption effects were mixed and CPI moved in an unexpected
  direction. Contract vintages and a positive-rate replicated design are still
  required before interpreting aggregate monetary transmission.
